How Much Should a Guest Post Cost in 2026?

Prices range from $5 to $5,000+, and most of that range is noise. Here's what real guest posts actually cost, based on studies of 250,000+ sites.

By the Freddy team · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

If you've shopped around for guest posts, you've seen a $15 gig on Fiverr and a $600 quote from an agency for what looks like the same thing. Both are "a guest post with a dofollow link." So what should you actually pay? We pulled the numbers from three large 2026 pricing studies to give you a straight answer.

The short answer

Across a study of 105,000+ sites, the median paid backlink costs about $140. But the average guest post runs higher, roughly $295 when you buy direct from a site, and $461 through a vendor (BuzzStream, 2026). The gap between "median" and "average" tells the whole story: a small number of high-authority sites pull the average up, while most placements sit well below it.

In other words: a fair price for a solid, real-traffic guest post in a normal niche is usually $100-$300. Pay much less and you're almost certainly buying from a PBN or link farm. Pay much more and you're either buying genuine top-tier authority, or paying for a brand name.

The number nobody talks about: traffic beats DR.
Every study found that a site's real organic traffic predicts its price, and its value, better than DR. Median price by traffic: under 1k visits/mo = $117; 1k-10k = $163; 10k-50k = $224; 250k+ = $386. A "DR 70" site with 200 visitors a month is worth far less than a DR 35 site pulling 15,000. Always check traffic, not just DR.

Guest post vs. niche edit

A guest post is a new article published on the host site with your link inside it. A niche edit (or link insertion) places your link into an existing, already-ranking article. Niche edits are usually cheaper and faster because there's no new content to write, expect roughly $60-$200 for a solid one, versus $100-$300+ for a guest post on a comparable site.

What managed services charge

If you'd rather not do the outreach yourself, managed providers price by DR tier. The mid-market (the credible, real-traffic providers) tends to land around:

  • DR 20+, ~$70-$105
  • DR 30+, ~$120-$150
  • DR 40+, ~$170-$220
  • DR 50+, ~$220-$400

The big-brand players charge noticeably more for the same tiers, you're paying for the name, not a better link.

Why the cheap option costs more

A $15 "DR 50" guest post is not the same product at a discount. It's almost always a private blog network (PBN), a site with fake or inflated metrics, or a page that will be deindexed within months. A link from a site nobody reads, or one Google already distrusts, does nothing for your rankings at best, and can actively hurt you at worst.

Before you buy any link, make sure the site has real organic traffic, a clean spam profile, and genuine topical relevance. We wrote a full checklist on this: How to Avoid Harmful Guest Posts →

What you should actually budget

For most businesses in a normal niche, a sensible target is $120-$250 per placement on a site with real traffic (DR 30-50, a few thousand monthly visitors). Go higher only when you specifically need top-tier authority. Go lower only if you can personally verify the site is legitimate.
